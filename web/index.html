Clean your Mac.
Keep it that way.

EasySweep shows where your space went — caches, app leftovers, duplicates, and developer data — and removes only what you approve. Set rules once, and it keeps routine clutter under control. Anything risky stays your call.

Coming soon to the Mac App Store
  • macOS 14 or later
  • Apple silicon and Intel

No account · No tracking · Safeguarded automation

Every app’s caches

One list for every cache.

Browsers, editors, chat apps and AI tools — grouped by app and sized to the byte, beside your developer data, app leftovers and macOS’s own clutter. Every item is measured and explained before you act.

Overview

Your whole disk at a glance. Pick Safe, Balanced or Deep and see exactly what each would take, section by section, before one click cleans it.

Caches

Every app’s caches in one list, grouped by app. Caches no installed app claims are listed apart and never ticked for you.

Developer

Xcode Derived Data, simulators, device support and runtimes, plus package manager and toolchain caches — each listed on its own, so you keep what you still need.

App Data

Downloaded AI models, session history and attachments — data that does not come back on its own, so it is flagged and never cleaned without you.

System

Logs, crash reports, downloaded wallpapers and update leftovers that macOS can rebuild, and that quietly pile up over time.

Four ways to see it

Draw every page as a list, tiles, a treemap or rings, with a plain or dot-matrix storage bar.

Uninstaller

Remove apps completely.

Every cache, container and preference an app leaves behind — found, shown with its evidence, and moved to the Trash with it.

Evidence, not guesses

Leftovers are grouped by how sure the match is. Files matched by the app’s identifier are ticked; name matches wait for you.

Always reversible

Everything goes to the Trash, and Undo puts it back where it was.

Shared files stay

A file another installed app also uses — a beta, a second copy — is marked shared and never removed.

Leftovers of deleted apps

Apps you dragged to the Trash yourself leave files too. EasySweep finds them, and tells you when an app is removed outside it.

Protected apps

Mark the apps that must never be uninstalled, Apple’s own included, and they are refused whole.

Sorted by size

Every installed app with its size, so the biggest are the first you see.

Utilities

Find what’s wasting space.

Tools for the space no app owns: your own files, old projects and forgotten startup items. Nothing is searched until you press Scan, and what you remove goes to the Trash.

Large & Old Files

Big files you haven’t opened in months, grouped by kind. Nothing is ticked for you — they are your files, and you decide.

Duplicate Files

Byte-identical copies, matched by content, not by name. One copy of every set is always kept: the oldest, the newest, or the one in a folder you prefer.

Finder Metadata

The .DS_Store files Finder leaves in every folder you open, found and cleared in one pass.

Project Caches

Build and tool caches inside your projects, by names that mean a cache everywhere — never node_modules, and never anything holding settings.

App Updates

See which apps are behind — from the App Store, Homebrew, MacPorts or their own updater — and update them from one list.

Startup Items

Review what opens at login and the helpers that run in the background, and remove the ones you never asked for.

Auto Clean

Set it once. It keeps up.

Rules that clean on their own when something grows too big or space runs low. Only what is safe ever runs unattended, and every run is recorded.

A trigger for every item

Clean a cache when it grows past its own size limit, or when free space drops below the level you choose — not one blunt rule for your entire Mac.

Keep what is still current

Remove everything or only files older than a day, week, or month. Recent work can stay exactly where it is.

Only what regenerates runs alone

A rule can reach only what comes back by itself. Archives, session history, and simulator devices always stay manual, whatever the rule says.

Waits for active apps

If a related app is running, unattended cleanup waits instead of removing files from underneath it.

Run it from the menu bar

Add Auto Clean buttons to the menu bar and run your rules on demand, without opening the window.

Follows you to every Mac

Your rules and categories sync over iCloud, so every Mac you use keeps the same setup.

Custom categories

Your own categories.

Downloads, projects, that one folder that keeps growing — make it a category and choose what goes.

A page of its own

Each category gets its own sidebar row, total, storage bar and Clean button, measured like any built-in section.

Clean by name, at any depth

Take every build or cache folder under a project folder by name, or pick folders by hand from a tree of what’s really there.

Levels you assign

Your folders are never ticked by Safe, Balanced, or Deep until you give them a level yourself.

Dark mode & Liquid Glass

Make it yours.

Liquid Glass in light or dark, dot-matrix bars, and every page as a list, tiles, a treemap or rings.

Liquid Glass

A translucent window that lets your desktop shine through.

Light or dark

Follow the system, or keep EasySweep light or dark whatever macOS does.

Your picture of the disk

A list, tiles, a treemap or rings on every page that cleans, and the storage bar as rows of dots.

Activity

See every gigabyte you got back.

Every clean, manual or automatic — how much, when, and from where — and every app update, in one history.

System Monitor

Your Mac, at a glance.

Free space, CPU, memory and disk activity — live in the window, and in the menu bar as widgets you choose.

Menu bar widgets

Pin free space, CPU, GPU, memory, network, disk activity and battery to the top of your screen, in the order you like.

A menu you arrange

Choose and order the cards in the menu bar dropdown, from free space to system health, plus your Auto Clean buttons.

Live charts

Processor, GPU, memory, network, and disk activity, updated every two seconds, with memory pressure as Activity Monitor reports it.

Act before space runs out

Choose your minimum free-space level, and EasySweep warns you before a full disk interrupts your day.

Every disk you use

External and other internal disks appear beside your startup disk, each opening onto the folders taking its space.

Quiet when you’re not looking

Nothing is sampled in the background. The monitor reads your Mac only while its page or menu is open.

Reclaim the space. Keep the control.

Scanning changes nothing. See what is using your space, understand why it is there, and remove only what you approve.
